# Source policy

Physical witnesses, museum artifacts, image records and abstract works are distinct entities. Association tables require source URLs; shared themes or similar titles do not authorize an association. Institutional witness metadata cites Yale's Beinecke Cipher Manuscript page, the National Library of Sweden's Codex Gigas page, and British Museum records for Papyrus Harris 500 and the D’Orbiney Papyrus. Their images are linked at the institution, not reproduced without reviewed rights. The Voynich record remains undeciphered and has no generated translation; Codex Gigas is a manuscript compilation, not a standalone scripture. Witness associations do not imply that an included edition was translated directly from that physical object.

Source depth describes what Atlas actually holds. A verbatim edition excerpt can be commentary, and fidelity does not imply historical truth. Canon membership, censorship, decipherment, attestation and scholarly agreement are separate fields. Future suppression records require an authority, location, date, reason and source; canon exclusion alone supplies none of those facts. Cultural protocols record community, recorder/context and reproduction permission independently of ordinary copyright.

Every displayed edition segment is verbatim text from a retained Project Gutenberg source file. Translation, commentary and primary composition are not equivalent; historical editions may contain introductory argument, notes, and outdated terminology. UI labels state this limitation and link to the exact source edition.

Taxonomy records are introductory navigation metadata, not completed ethnographies, exclusive membership rules, canon assignments or claims that a broad label is a single institution. Every new taxonomy node and edge carries a source URL. The initial expansion uses museum, university, community/institutional and scholarly public-education sources; institutional self-description remains distinguishable from independent scholarship. Chinese Buddhism and Horn traditions intentionally support multiple navigation parents. No text is assigned to a branch without separate review, and public Indigenous/Oceanic sources do not authorize ingestion of restricted ceremonial knowledge.

Unresolved research-queue records are private curation leads, not Atlas facts. They are excluded from public routes, search and model synthesis. A lead can reach publication status only from provisional resolution and only with at least one HTTPS source, an explicit publication target, and completed rights, cultural and scholarly review marked cleared or not required. Rejected and disputed leads retain their status history rather than becoming hidden evidence for a claim.

Passage `contentType` is independent of a work's religious authority or historical truth. Exact KJV named-book extractions are labeled primary text for that edition. The bundled Gilgamesh and Egyptian Book of the Dead sources are scholarly studies and remain commentary even where they quote ancient material. All other mixed historical-edition segments stay Unknown until segment-level review distinguishes translated source text, introductions, notes, commentary, footnotes and appendices. Unknown material remains discoverable but is never silently relabeled scripture.

Egyptian corpus and literature metadata added during Phase 5 uses cited university and museum resources, including UCL Digital Egypt for Universities pages and the British Museum collection catalog, together with retained Project Gutenberg source files. A corpus-level place name does not authorize a point coordinate, and a period assigned to a collection does not automatically date each surviving witness. Nineteen bounded historical English editions are ingested through explicit, occurrence-aware source boundaries. The Creation of the Gods preserves its two printed recensions as source sections of one work, and the Metternich Stela material remains one composite corpus rather than being inflated into separate works for its internal chapters. Translator introductions and remarks are excluded from the selected ranges; retained source footnotes are classified separately as `Footnote`, structural headings remain `Unknown`, and translated composition blocks are `Primary text`. Incomplete surviving texts remain marked incomplete even when the available translation is fully preserved. Taking of Joppa remains `Unknown` because its reconstructed boundary is unavailable in the retained plain text. Setna I keeps its reconstructed opening and bracketed editorial summary `Unknown` while separately classifying the remaining translation. The Budge editions are historical translations with outdated terminology and scholarship; Project Gutenberg also labels the 7413 digitization an early, unsatisfactory edition needing further work, and each derived edition exposes the applicable limitation.

The 41 additional Egyptian research records cite HTTPS university, museum or academic-publication metadata and bundle no source text. Unsupported composition dates, witness dates and point coordinates remain null. A named corpus is retained as one research record unless the cited evidence supports separately modeled compositions; recurring genres such as harpists’ songs, protective amulet texts and magical formularies are not counted as fabricated individual works. Descriptions distinguish literary settings from historical-event evidence and identify royal victory inscriptions as interested, propagandistic sources. Metadata availability does not imply translation availability, completeness, canon status or permission to reproduce a witness.

The catalog names and regional groupings originate in the supplied specification. These groupings are navigation aids rather than claims that each is a coherent religious tradition. Sparse records are explicitly unreviewed. Dates for enriched records are provisional editorial estimates and must not be used as independently established scholarly conclusions. Object dates come from the cited museum records and apply to the objects only.

Relationship records require a source URL, a relationship type, chronology notes, differences, and an uncertainty category. A thematic reading link is not proof of influence. No records are rated B or C without a supporting scholarly review.

Image provenance: Met object records 324794 and 74875; both marked public domain in the Met Collection API at build time. Open Access policy: https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access. Flood reading-path context: https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/flood-stories.

Egyptian image intake contains the six initial Met Book of the Dead objects (548344, 553673, 551786, 590941, 553720 and 550820) plus the 24 explicitly reviewed objects in `data/intake/met-egypt-reviewed.json`. The reviewed expansion excludes human remains, records a sensitivity decision and protocol-review note for each object, and deliberately includes papyri, ostraca and an inscribed relief rather than treating every writable object as a manuscript witness. Together the 30 Met objects provide 103 verified Egyptian images. Each accepted API response asserted `isPublicDomain: true`, every required image URL returned an image content type, and raw institutional metadata is retained in `data/provenance/met` with a source hash and verification timestamp. The importer validates the full reviewed batch before replacing seed data and preserves the timestamp when a semantically identical API response only reorders metadata. Witness type is an explicit review decision where supplied; conservative fallback inference excludes blank sheets. Import uses explicit IDs, and the optional Book of the Dead association checks the institutional title, object name and description. An institution's work identification does not establish a translation's textual dependency. Met Open Access status does not grant permissions for another institution or override cultural restrictions.

Mesopotamian source intake uses retained subsets of the ORACC ePSD2 literary corpus and ORACC Q Catalogue. The ingestion script verifies the source archives' embedded CC0 metadata and committed SHA-256 values before regenerating Atlas records. ORACC Q identifiers describe cataloged compositions or corpus groupings; CDLI P identifiers describe physical objects. Atlas never converts a Q identifier into a tablet witness or counts internal series tablets, episodes or line groups as separate compositions merely to raise a total.

The 26 searchable Mesopotamian editions preserve ePSD2 scholarly composite transliteration forms and ORACC line labels. They contain no bundled English translation. `Primary text` identifies exact composition content in this source edition; it does not mean that every sign survives on one physical object, resolve damaged or restored passages, or establish the historicity of a literary claim. Composite transliterations and physical tablet witnesses remain visibly separate. Ten CDLI witnesses provide object-level P-number references where reviewed composition associations are available, while their records explicitly avoid claiming that every displayed composite line occurs on that object.

The Anunna / Anunnaki research data preserves nine exact ORACC attestations and normalizes Anunna, Anuna, Anunnaki and Anunnaku only as reviewed search aliases. Mainstream scholarship, alternative interpretations and modern popular claims occupy separate source-bounded records. Publisher pages for modern books establish that those books and claims exist; they are not evidence that ancient cuneiform supports the claims. Comparison rows state the ancient evidence available, the modern assertion and the limitation. Modern claims are excluded from source passages and cannot outrank ancient attestations in Scholarly search.

Louvre Gudea cylinder pages and CDLI object pages supply institution-specific witness context; ORACC RINAP and Q Catalogue metadata supply corpus scope where used. A linked object does not create an edition dependency, and an institution's catalog statement does not waive another repository's copyright or cultural restrictions. The stricter historical-evidence, copyright, provenance, cultural-access, safety and non-fabrication rules in the project specifications continue to govern every record.

Chinese source intake retains 30 Project Gutenberg ebooks identified as public domain in the United States. Atlas preserves their Chinese text exactly in sequence, records raw-file hashes and labels non-Chinese structural blocks `Unknown`. A `Primary text` label means the block contains the transmitted Chinese work in that digitized edition; it does not certify a critical edition, a specific physical witness, an English translation or historical truth. Chinese Text Project and CBETA links on metadata-only records support discovery and scope only; Atlas does not reproduce their content under Project Gutenberg's license.

Chinese titles, characters, pinyin and school/community labels are navigation metadata. “China” does not imply one religion, canon or lineage. Confucian / Ru, Daoist, Buddhist, Mohist, Legalist, School of Names, folk-religious, Manichaean, Jingjiao, Islamic and syncretic records remain separately filterable. Four Dunhuang object records cite the International Dunhuang Programme and remain independent of the bundled transcriptions; institutional image rights are not inferred or reproduced.

## Phase 10 witness expansion

The witness archive adds 18 physical records across the Dead Sea Scrolls, major biblical codices, Nag Hammadi, Derveni, Oxyrhynchus, Herculaneum, Elephantine, Mawangdui, Guodian, Gandharan, Gilgit and Mesoamerican manuscripts. Work links are made only when the institutional record identifies the content or the record explicitly documents the association. Unknown dates remain null, and collection membership never supplies a guessed composition, recension or findspot. Rights statements describe repository terms rather than implying that public metadata makes images or modern transcriptions reusable. Codex Vaticanus links the Vatican Library's verified IIIF manifest; the manifest remains at the repository.

## Phase 11 unresolved-writing policy

The unresolved-writing collection keeps four questions separate: whether marks form a script, whether sign values are known, whether a language is identified, and whether semantic translation is possible. A readable script can encode an unknown language; a partly understood accounting system need not have known phonetic values; and a securely dated object can still be semantically unreadable. Corpus pages therefore expose distinct states instead of flattening every case into “undeciphered.”

Decipherment proposals are claim records, never primary-source translations. Each admitted hypothesis records its proponent, publication context, proposed language and values, method, bilingual anchor, claimed scope, peer-review state, independent replication, criticism, reception and confidence. Repository and scholarly links establish the evidence boundary; Atlas does not reproduce protected images or turn an external proposed reading into a bundled edition. Questions that request a glyph meaning, translation, author, religion, deity, Jesus, aliens or Atlantis from one of these corpora return the evidence states and disagreement without model synthesis.

## Phase 12 esoteric and literary policy

Grimoires, ritual-magic texts, Hermetic philosophy, alchemical literature, demonological polemic and religiously influential literature remain distinct source genres. Cataloging a described spirit, operation or accusation records historical textual content; it does not establish supernatural efficacy, a real entity, or the truth of an accusation. Atlas does not convert this material into step-by-step ritual, coercive or harmful instructions. The Malleus Maleficarum is contextualized as witch-trial polemic with a history of misogyny and persecution rather than treated as reliable evidence of witchcraft.

Literary works are marked `LITERATURE / CULTURAL INFLUENCE — NON-SCRIPTURAL` wherever they appear in the Phase 12 collection. Biblical characters, afterlife settings or theological themes do not make Dante, Goethe, Marlowe, Milton, Byron or Anatole France scriptural authorities. Complete Project Gutenberg bodies are preserved as historical editions with their own attribution, language, license, source URL and checksum. Five repository records demonstrate the witness layer: British Library Sloane MSS 3648 and 3825, British Library Yates Thompson MS 36, Herzog August Bibliothek Cod. Guelf. 47.13 Aug. 4°, and Wellcome Collection EPB/INC/4.a.12. Those objects are not asserted as the source copies for the bundled editions.

Practice records preserve a nineteen-term vocabulary and use narrower source-specific labels in each case. Mesopotamian ancestor care is not necromancy; an oracle bone is not generic spirit contact; an Asmat object description does not authorize a universal or current practice claim. Operational text is excluded. The separate sacrifice dataset uses all nine required evidence categories. Carthage retains paired disagreement, Caesar's Druid account remains a conqueror's allegation, blood libel is explicitly false, a United Nations-reported allegation is not an adjudicated fact, and broad ritual-abuse-network claims remain unverified. No graphic content is bundled. Organization records establish historically documented identities and discontinuities without creating membership edges, reproducing private rites, or treating alleged successors as the original body.

## Phase 13 modern-religions evidence policy

Modern-religion profiles are bounded discovery records, not claims that each movement has one uncontested canon, authority or identity. Institutional self-description, independent academic history, government and court records, criticism, institutional responses, former-member accounts, journalism and unverified claims remain distinct evidence categories. A source can establish what its author, institution or court said without establishing every underlying allegation as fact. Official sources are visibly identified as first-party sources; they do not substitute for independent analysis.

Legal records retain jurisdiction, docket or case reference, decision date and disposition when the cited source supplies them. A ruling is summarized only within its actual scope. Criticism and a corresponding institutional response are separately traceable rather than merged into a synthetic verdict. Former-member accounts document personal testimony and are not generalized to every member. Media reports remain journalism. Unverified claims are labeled `Unverified`, state the evidence limit and cannot be promoted through repetition or proximity to verified records.

The Scientology collection separates official teaching from historical analysis, legal records, criticism, Church responses and bibliography. Its books, creed and codes remain metadata-only because their inclusion requirement does not override copyright. Publication chronology distinguishes first publication from lecture or article antecedents where necessary. The collection may link to official descriptions, library catalogs, court repositories and independent scholarship, while the source's perspective and evidentiary role remain visible.

Claims of ancient, extraterrestrial, channeled or otherwise nonordinary origin are recorded separately from documented composition and publication dates. Oahspe, The Urantia Book, A Course in Miracles, the Law of One/Ra Material and the Seth Material remain dated to their modern documentary history in Atlas. A claimed origin is part of a movement's or publisher's account and is not converted into an ancient date. The same rule applies to modern revelations and reception claims across Bahá’í, Latter Day Saint, Cao Dai, Tenrikyo, Falun Gong, Thelema, New Age and UFO-religion records.

Umbrella labels preserve internal distinctions. Modern Spiritualism is not collapsed into French Spiritism; Luciferian traditions are not treated as synonyms for Satanism; the Church of Satan and The Satanic Temple remain separate organizations; New Age and UFO religions are analytic groupings rather than single churches; and Rastafari is not assigned a universal central authority. Nation of Islam is included as a distinct modern movement. Sensitive records, including Heaven's Gate, use non-graphic contextual warnings and omit operational method details.

## Phase 14 forbidden, lost and disputed evidence policy

A suppression record requires a cited act or policy and separately records the responsible authority, jurisdiction, start and end where known, stated reason and present status. Atlas does not infer prohibition from canon exclusion, rejection, controversy, restricted teaching or later unavailability. The Shepherd of Hermas is a canon-boundary case labeled “Canon boundary / not banned.” The rabbinic limits discussed for chariot and creation exposition are labeled “Audience-restricted teaching / not a book ban.” A historical restriction is not presented as current unless the cited evidence establishes that status.

Lost-work records identify how a work is known: direct fragments, quotations, summaries, titles, catalogs or later testimony. Each record keeps the attesting source, surviving material, source chain and reconstruction level visible. Dependent witnesses do not become independent confirmation. Berossus's _Babyloniaca_, for example, is described through dependent and branching later witnesses rather than as an extant complete edition. Fragmentary records may describe or cite verified surviving material within source and rights limits, but Atlas supplies no missing passages.

Hypothetical sources and reconstructed religious complexes record arguments for and against a scholarly model. They have no physical witness, no quotation policy beyond `none`, no translation eligibility, and no reader edition. Reconstructed outlines, vocabulary or comparative models are scholarship about a proposal; they are not recovered documents or verbatim ancient speech.

Disputed records separate claimed date, authorship and provenance from material dating, find history and current assessment. Evidence cited in support when one exists, evidence against, reception history, majority and minority assessments, and later historiography remain attributed and separate. The Gospel of Jesus' Wife record states that tests consistent with an ancient papyrus or ink do not establish that the writing itself is ancient. A legal acquittal does not authenticate the Jehoash inscription, and exposure of the Kinderhook plates or Salamander Letter as forgeries does not adjudicate the truth of a religion.

## Phase 15 ontology and entity evidence policy

Ontology headings are comparative discovery tools. A parent/child or secondary concept edge does not establish that communities define a concept identically. Original-language terms retain language, script, transliteration, semantic relation, scope and source; a gloss, related term or contrast is not promoted to an unconditional synonym. In particular, anattā remains a source-scoped contrast in the soul concept record rather than a “soul” alias.

Entity names are source-specific assertions, separate from contextual relationships. A translation rendering, later identification, group association, rejected identification or historical conflation cannot enter the alias inventory unless the cited evidence supports that name for the entity in the stated scope. Belial and Baal remain distinct. Lucifer and Satan remain distinct and are connected only through a cited later contextual conflation. Institution renamings and council identities retain their own periods and source context.

An entity occurrence requires an attested form, source, locator, language/script context, role description and confidence. Nine Anunna records reproduce retained ORACC composite transliterations but do not claim that a composite is one physical tablet or supply an uncited translation. Evolution stages describe changes visible in cited sources; they do not imply a single inevitable development or erase competing interpretations.

Conflict-causation and conversion/coercion vocabularies are controlled classification choices. Every applied value requires its own case-specific evidence, confidence and source links. The initial cases use OpenStax historical syntheses alongside primary or archival evidence from the Yale Avalon Project, Fordham Internet History Sourcebooks and the UK National Archives; a Cambridge peer-reviewed study supplies counterevidence to an instantaneous Safavid conversion narrative. Religious difference alone does not establish religious causation, conquest does not establish forced conversion, and state support does not establish population conversion. Mixed causes, gradual change, continuity and disputed classification remain available instead of forcing a single label. Constantinian legalization and sponsorship is therefore not classified as forced conversion. The Safavid case uses forced-conversion and conversion-under-threat only for measures directly supported by the cited evidence, while separately recording Sunni continuity and uncertainty about later adoption.

## Phase 16 question and comparative-research evidence policy

Question-engine evidence enters model context only after search scope, public-domain availability and cultural/AI eligibility checks. Passages classified `Unknown` retain an explicit unclassified evidence role and are AI-ineligible; Atlas does not relabel them as primary text, translation/editorial evidence or commentary. Provider output names exact canonical source IDs and verbatim source excerpts; Atlas resolves the metadata and rejects absent, misquoted or role-mismatched citations. An exact quotation is still insufficient when it does not entail the claim. Every provider-authored claim, including uncertainty and retrieval-status claims, must have citations and an evidence ladder. The independent audit must cover every provider-authored claim with separate scope, source-role and confidence decisions, and must return a separate entailment decision for every resolved citation ID even when two citations point to the same source. No provider-controlled section, claim type, status or prose can grant an uncited exception. Atlas's own deterministic evidence-only retrieval notice is created outside provider synthesis.

The “Earliest surviving evidence currently represented in Atlas” section may contain a claim only when its cited canonical evidence identifies a physical witness and witness date, states date confidence, uses the exact “currently represented in Atlas” scope and includes a survival-bias note. A catalog work composition range is provisional contextual chronology, not evidence for the date of the oldest surviving witness, and cannot support that section by itself. If Atlas lacks qualifying witness evidence, the section remains empty rather than inferring survival chronology.

The A–E scale retains its original evidentiary meanings. A primary-text quotation establishes attestation in that edition, not historicity. B and C require scholarly evidence; D is reserved for meaningful scholarly dispute; E marks speculation; `Unreviewed` is a workflow state. Attestation, authenticity, decipherment, canon, access and historicity remain separate axes. All 12 neutrality checks run on generated answers, including chronology, alternatives, oral-tradition framing, Western category defaults and religious or skeptical superiority. Failed output is regenerated and re-audited once, then discarded in favor of evidence-only output.

Streaming emits a retrieval-started status before lexical or semantic retrieval and may then expose verified canonical evidence and process status before generation finishes. It may not expose model-authored claims until all citation, claim and neutrality checks pass. Cancellation is accepted from the beginning of the request and propagates through bundled-corpus indexing, lexical retrieval, database retrieval, semantic retrieval, provider generation, provider audit and the response stream. Unresolved writing systems use a dedicated safeguard status: without inspectable canonical spans, Atlas emits no evidence-verification claim and no uncited historical, decipherment or hypothesis detail. A text, edition, tradition, date or content-type filter excludes unresolved archive metadata that cannot satisfy that scope.

Opposition stances are proposition-linked, attributed records with context, confidence, uncertainty and source IDs. The nine controlled categories remain distinct: doctrinal opposition, philosophical critique, internal heterodox opposition, skeptical critique, materialist critique, alternative religious interpretation, mystical reinterpretation, adversarial reinterpretation and modern secular critique. Compare cases keep similarity, contradiction, chronology, documented influence and alternative interpretation separate; resemblance never creates an influence edge. Teaching vs Historical Conduct cites teaching and conduct independently, names actors and mixed motives, retains supporting and opposing voices, and never treats adherent conduct as authoritative doctrine or issues a theological verdict.

Every published static stance, comparison finding and Teaching vs Historical Conduct lane identifies the source span inspected for that individual claim and carries an explicit human-review state. A whole-book or home-page URL without a locator is not claim-level support. Where a reviewed mapping to an exact bundled segment has been established, a static finding also retains the canonical passage ID and that passage's existing content classification. Absence of `canonicalPassageIds` means the locator cites the external edition or source span and does not claim a mapping to an Atlas passage. Dhammapada verse 5 and Bhagavad Gita 5:18 therefore remain historical-translation selections classified `Unknown`; the research layer does not promote them to reviewed primary-text status.

Rights and cultural rules apply before model input, embedding, direct passage reads, saved collections, print/PDF output and JSON, CSV or Markdown export. Rights eligibility requires affirmative edition reuse terms and non-conflicting work-level rights metadata; edition presence alone never implies public-domain status. Nested saved payloads are inspected rather than trusting an outer finding ID. Comparison and question saves use bounded, versioned provenance snapshots with IDs, locators, source roles and URLs instead of copying passage bodies. A shared physical witness or image receives a work-scoped source ID so multiple linked works cannot create duplicate citation identities.

Curated pack slots carry one of five evidence states: evidence-ready, unclassified selection, metadata-only, missing-source or culturally restricted. Only evidence-ready selections support comparative findings. A bounded passage whose source role is `Unknown` remains an unclassified selection and can appear only as context beside a finding or in a non-evidentiary coverage note. Metadata establishes that a work or source exists, not what it says. Culturally restricted slots remain discoverable as bounded metadata but are excluded from excerpts, AI input and exports, including provenance snapshots and their referenced output IDs. Ethiopian canon evidence remains community-specific; Meqabyan is not collapsed into Greek Maccabees. Ancient Anunna attestations, modern alternative scholarship and Sitchin or ancient-astronaut claims retain distinct source categories. Indigenous Creation preserves community identity and permission state instead of universalizing a single Indigenous narrative.
