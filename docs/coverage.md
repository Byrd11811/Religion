The image archive contains 105 verified images across 33 artifact records, including 103 images of 30 Met Egyptian objects and one metadata-only British Museum artifact. Detailed major-release gaps and numerical milestones are tracked in `expansion-progress.md`.

The corpus contains 600 work and research records and 124 source editions covering 121 works, with 31,484 searchable segments. Eighty-eight works have complete named texts or bounded digitized editions. Ancient Egypt accounts for 72 reviewed work/research records, of which 20 have searchable source content. Mesopotamia accounts for 77 reviewed composition/corpus records, of which 27 are searchable: 26 ORACC Sumerian composite transliterations and the pre-existing Gilgamesh study. Metadata-only research records do not count as editions. Gilgamesh and the Egyptian Book of the Dead provide historical studies and translated selections, visibly labeled as partial coverage. Regional coverage remains uneven; absence from this corpus is not absence from a tradition.

The structured Question Explorer exposes 15 answer sections. Evidence-only operation works without a model. Optional provider output must resolve exact canonical IDs and source excerpts, then pass deterministic neutrality checks and an independent audit covering every provider-authored claim's scope, source role and confidence plus an individual entailment decision for every resolved citation ID. The audit requires exact claim and citation-ID coverage, including distinct citations that share a source, and also covers all 12 original neutrality items. `Unknown` passages remain unclassified and AI-ineligible. Evidence-only Primary Evidence cards contain primary sources only; eligible translator/editor and scholarly retrieval records retain their actual roles in the source index. “Earliest surviving evidence currently represented in Atlas” is available only when the cited evidence supplies a physical witness ID and date, a date-specific precision/basis assessment and a survival-bias note; work composition ranges provide contextual chronology but cannot satisfy that section. General attestation labels such as `Securely attested` cannot stand in for date confidence. Searchable works acquire witness or artifact context only through explicit curated work links with public HTTPS provenance. The production adapter selects the earliest dated secure linked witness in the current Atlas catalog, keeps its object chronology separate from composition, labels the date as an approximate/range or catalog-stated repository date without claiming that Atlas independently redated it, and exposes a verified public-domain institutional image only when its artifact and witness chain, attribution and explicit rights metadata resolve. Images and Artifacts claims require primary object evidence with a canonical witness or artifact ID; Atlas does not infer interpretation or rights from pixels.
