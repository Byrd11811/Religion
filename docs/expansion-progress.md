# Depth expansion: audit and first implementation

The user-supplied expansion brief is preserved in `expansion-spec.md`. It extends the baseline product specification; its numerical goals are milestones, not claims about current coverage.

## Audit

Baseline: Next.js App Router, React client shell, TypeScript, PGlite/PostgreSQL and optional pgvector retrieval. There are 252 legacy catalog records, 44 source editions across 43 works, 20,991 preserved segments, two museum images, three editorial reading guides and two textual-tension cases. The app already supports reading, citations, comparison, map, timeline, canon browsing, research collections and exports. The original tables and public routes remain intact.

The main gaps are scholarly metadata depth, physical-witness associations, original-language transcriptions, a broader independently reviewed relationship corpus, comprehensive cultural coverage and image intake. Legacy passages can contain edition commentary; they must not all be classified automatically as ancient primary evidence. The browser still receives the legacy metadata catalog and the legacy lexical index remains memory-based. The new archive endpoint is database-paginated, but this does not complete the long-term search scalability work.

## Implemented foundation

- Additive work, recension, witness, artifact and image tables, with evidence records, edition/witness associations, cultural protocols, community-specific canon memberships and documented suppression events.
- Existing work IDs retained through `legacy_text_id`; original passages, edition IDs and user collection storage unchanged. Migration tests confirm repeated synchronization retains research notes and IDs.
- Typed work profiles with source depth, coverage, decipherment, access and review status. Existing uncertain metadata stays unreviewed. No manuscript association is inferred from a title or decorative image.
- Database-paginated `/api/works` and `/library/deep-archive`, filtering by title and source coverage, with work links through `/works/[id]`.
- Physical-witness browsing through `/library/manuscripts`, `/witnesses/[id]`, and compatible `/manuscripts/[id]` links. The separate `/library/undeciphered` research lab links Voynich MS 408 to its physical record while keeping corpus-level decipherment evidence distinct. Codex Gigas remains modeled as a manuscript compilation.
- Artifact pages and separately attributed images. Two existing Met images retained with no invented work/witness associations.
- CoverageDepthBadge, EvidenceStatusBadge, DeciphermentBadge, WitnessCard, ArtifactCard, ManuscriptGallery, ImageAttribution, EvidenceLadder, PrimaryVsInterpretationTabs and KnownClaimedUnknownPanel.
- Citation drawers expose source depth and the evidence ladder. Optional synthesis refuses ineligible lost, hypothetical, restricted and unresolved-script records.

## Migration and verification

Stop the running app before maintenance. Run `npm run db:migrate` to add and populate the archive model. Fresh embedded databases initialize automatically; server PostgreSQL requires the explicit migration command. The legacy-derived profiles are maintained by `syncArchive`; do not edit their generated metadata directly in SQL. Future curated enrichment needs a separate provenance-preserving overlay.

Run typecheck, lint, core tests, browser tests and production build. The new tests cover repeated migration, source eligibility, image provenance, archive filtering and pagination, witness category separation and mobile layout. Model-provider services remain optional and were not called.

Verified for this milestone: 14 core tests and nine browser tests pass; lint, TypeScript and production build pass. Browser checks confirm that museum images load, image navigation changes the selection and zoom is keyboard-operable. API response snapshots retain their original bytes in Git for source-hash verification.

## Phase 16 — Question, opposition and comparison engines

The Question Explorer now emits a retrieval-started event before lexical or semantic retrieval, then streams canonical evidence and progress through a cancellable NDJSON endpoint before returning a final structured answer. Cancellation propagates from retrieval through provider generation and audit, and request generations prevent buffered events, errors or completion cleanup from an older stream from overwriting a replacement query. All 15 required sections exist even when empty. Every provider-authored claim, including uncertainty and retrieval-status claims, requires exact canonical source IDs and excerpts, explicit evidence type, confidence, reasoning, alternatives and a source-linked evidence ladder. A separate audit covers every provider-authored claim for scope, source-role use and confidence, gives every resolved citation ID its own entailment decision even when citations share a source, and returns all 12 original neutrality decisions. Exact claim/citation-ID equality is enforced. `Unknown` passages remain explicitly unclassified and AI-ineligible. Evidence-only Primary Evidence cards contain only primary sources; retrieved translator/editor and scholarly records remain visible under their actual roles in the source index. The “Earliest surviving evidence currently represented in Atlas” section requires cited physical-witness identity and date, a date-specific precision/basis assessment, the exact Atlas scope and a survival-bias note; catalog work composition ranges and general witness-attestation labels cannot support it. The production adapter adds witness and artifact context only through explicit curated work links with public HTTPS provenance. Its date assessment distinguishes approximate/range catalog dates from catalog-stated dates and explicitly says Atlas has not independently redated the object. The Images and Artifacts section requires primary object evidence with a canonical witness or artifact ID. A verified image contributes its catalog metadata and explicit rights statement, never a pixel-derived interpretation or inferred license. One regeneration and re-audit is allowed; malformed, missing, invalid, unrelated or rejected support falls back to evidence-only output. Atlas may add its own deterministic uncited evidence-gap notice only after audited synthesis has been rejected or is unavailable. Unresolved-corpus requests use a distinct safeguard state and expose no uncited historical or interpretive detail; active source filters apply before the safeguard reveals metadata.

Opposition retrieval uses four reviewed propositions and 23 attributed stances across the nine controlled categories: doctrinal opposition, philosophical critique, internal heterodox opposition, skeptical critique, materialist critique, alternative religious interpretation, mystical reinterpretation, adversarial reinterpretation and modern secular critique. A tradition name never creates a stance. Compare exposes six distinct backend modes: synthesis, similarities, contradictions, earlier parallels, later echoes and alternative interpretations. Its four reviewed cases preserve proposition and context, chronology, evidence gaps and influence boundaries. Filter predicates now run before lexical and semantic candidate limits.

Teaching vs Historical Conduct provides four cases across Christian, Islamic, Buddhist and Hindu contexts. Each case separately cites teaching, conduct, actors/institutions, contemporary justification and texts, supporting and opposing religious voices, nonreligious motives, later theology and modern scholarship. The interface states that adherent or institutional conduct is not automatically doctrine and applies the same lane model to every case.

All seven required curated packs are discoverable at `/compare/packs` and their stable detail routes. They contain 72 requested slots and 21 authored outputs, split into 10 evidence-backed findings and 11 coverage/protocol notes. Seventeen slots are evidence-ready, 17 are bounded but unclassified, 23 metadata-only, 14 culturally restricted and one missing-source.
