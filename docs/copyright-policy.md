# Copyright and cultural permissions

Bundled text files are public-domain historical editions in the USA from Project Gutenberg, with full source notices retained. Passage copies reference their source edition and translator. No claim is made that an ancient work makes every translation public domain. Check applicable jurisdictional restrictions before redistribution.

The Met photographs are CC0 1.0 Universal / Open Access. The app hotlinks the verified image URLs and displays object titles, dates, creator information, institution, license and source links. Images are not silently associated with unrelated primary works. OpenStreetMap tiles retain their attribution and require an internet connection.

Copyrighted modern texts and unapproved oral-tradition transcriptions are metadata-only. Restricted cultural material requires community-sensitive selection and permission before ingestion. User notes and collections remain local unless exported or explicitly sent to a configured service. The app makes no public redistribution or user-upload licensing assumption.

## Modern religions and new religious movements

Phase 13 treats modern publication as the default copyright boundary. Scientology books, creeds and codes; contemporary institutional translations; and other twentieth- and twenty-first-century religious publications are represented through bibliographic metadata, concise editorial summaries and outbound source links. Atlas does not ingest their full text, create a reader edition, or imply that an official web page grants republication rights.

Rights are evaluated per edition. A historical work may be public domain in the United States while a current translation, edited text, facsimile, typography, image or institutional mark remains protected. The 1830 Book of Mormon edition already bundled in Atlas remains available under its reviewed public-domain edition record; adding an LDS movement profile does not replace or downgrade that source. Historical Theosophical, Spiritualist, New Thought, Anthroposophical, Luciferian and Thelemic material requires the same edition-specific review before any future full-text intake.

External quotations are kept short and attributed. A title, publication date, source description or link documents the existence and provenance of a work; it does not authorize redistribution. Where an institution asserts its own copyright or use terms, the stricter stated restriction remains visible in the record. Atlas does not infer public-domain status from a work's religious, canonical or historical importance.
